Structural Variants

Steps 04, 19, 18, 22, 05 · Complete · file date 2021-03-04 05:06 UTC · link to this section

Filtered: e-value below 0.01, under half of the reads with mapping quality 0 (q0) and under half N bases (pN), no overlap with a centromere or heterochromatin band, and no chrY call when the declared sex is female.

Short reads find large deletions and duplications better than insertions and inversions, and the callers disagree on many events. The totals count every call, most of them common in the population; only the AnnotSV class 4 and 5 rows are graded for disease.

Structural Variants, Class 4 and 5 (AnnotSV)

A class 4 or 5 SV made by one caller, a deletion on the exon edges of a transcript (the reads of a processed transcript or cDNA aligned across an intron) or one at 1% or more in AnnotSV's benign set is unlikely to be a rare pathogenic SV: check the reads before acting on it (docs/05-annotsv.md). Step 05's rules (assets/sv_rule_regions.tsv and the read-depth check) move a class 4 or 5 call to class 3 when it lies in a coding tandem repeat, an immunoglobulin or T-cell receptor locus or the MHC, names a pseudogene by a disease gene's alias, or its read depth does not show the change; those calls are listed apart with the reason and are not counted here.

Alu, L1 and SVA copies the genome carries and the reference does not, found and genotyped by MEGAnE (Kojima et al. 2023) from short read pairs. Most are common and harmless; the catalog rows are insertions known to cause disease. Not callable: too few reads in that window to tell. Not seen is not proof of absence: short reads find an SVA's edges, not its body. Repeat Expansions

Step 09 · Complete · file date 2021-03-04 05:06 UTC · link to this section

Not graded: the repeat catalog gives these loci no thresholds (for MUC1, CEL, TYMS and PLIN4 the reference repeat is longer than 300 bases, so no short read spans it), so Stranger gives them no status. Not graded is not normal (docs/09-str-expansions.md).

RFC1: Stranger's catalog sizes the degenerate AARRG unit and flags a low count, so the motif decides. The motif count reads the reads at the locus; it cannot size a long expansion.

Repeat-expansion calls from short reads can be wrong at some loci, and a long expansion cannot be sized. Disease thresholds: docs/results/structural-and-repeats.md.

Clonal Haematopoiesis (CHIP)

Step 49 · Complete · file date 2021-03-04 05:06 UTC · link to this section

Clonal haematopoiesis is common with age and most people with it never get a blood cancer: a call here is not a diagnosis. A call is a change in a CHIP gene's published classes, found by Mutect2 without a matched normal; one at a VAF of 35% or more is left out as possibly inherited. docs/49-chip.md says how to read it.

Mosaic Chromosomal Alterations (MoChA)

Step 51 · Complete · file date 2021-03-04 05:06 UTC · link to this section

Large gains, losses and copy-neutral losses of heterozygosity (CN-LOH) carried by a share of the blood cells, from MoChA on the phased heterozygous sites of chr1 to chr22. In a UK Biobank study they became more common with age and went with a higher risk of a later blood cancer (Loh et al. 2018). That is a population-level association, not a diagnosis; an event is confirmed on a new blood sample. Below the floor nothing is reported, and an event above it can still be missed. SMN1/SMN2 Copy Number (Parascopy)

The other loci of step 35's catalog (assets/paralog_loci.tsv): agCN is the copies of the gene and its paralogs together, read only with filter PASS and quality 20 or more; which copy gained or lost one is not said. Not assessed names the reason (most often: no read over the locus).

Parascopy reads short reads at SMN1 and SMN2 and at the other loci of its catalog. A quality under 20 or a filter other than PASS leaves a copy unresolved. HBA1 and HBA2, CYP21A2 and the gene-dosage loci have no Parascopy model: step 35 counts them from read depth against flanking sequence (CYP21A2 also from six sites where it differs from its pseudogene CYP21A1P), which cannot say which chromosome lost or gained a copy. The copy-number intervals (ClinGen's recurrent CNV regions, carrier deletion alleles, the AZF regions) are read from depth too. A research estimate, not a diagnostic test: confirm a result with a clinical laboratory (MLPA or qPCR; gap-PCR for alpha-thalassaemia).

Runs of Homozygosity

Step 11 · Complete · file date 2021-03-04 05:06 UTC · link to this section

Long runs concentrated on one chromosome, with few elsewhere, are the sign of isodisomy: both copies of that chromosome from one parent. They can also be chance in a child of related parents. Which parent gave both copies cannot be told without the parents' samples, and a heterodisomy (two different copies from one parent) leaves no runs at all. The parents' genotypes confirm a UPD. Where an imprinted region is listed, a methylation test of that region tests for its imprinting disorder; on its own it does not prove a UPD (docs/11-roh-analysis.md).

F_ROH is the share of the autosomes (outside the bands) in runs of 5 MB or more.

Autosomes only: chrX and chrY are left out, and so is the part of a segment in a centromere or heterochromatin band (GRCh38 cytoband acen, gvar, stalk), where few markers map. No population allele frequencies were used (bcftools roh --AF-dflt 0.4): read the total and the short segments as rough; the long segments are the signal.
