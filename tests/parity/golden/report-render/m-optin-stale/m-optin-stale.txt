  Long runs concentrated on one chromosome, with few elsewhere, are the sign of isodisomy: both copies of that chromosome from one parent. They can also be chance in a child of related parents. Which parent gave both copies cannot be told without the parents' samples, and a heterodisomy (two different copies from one parent) leaves no runs at all. The parents' genotypes confirm a UPD. Where an imprinted region is listed, a methylation test of that region tests for its imprinting disorder; on its own it does not prove a UPD (docs/11-roh-analysis.md).
