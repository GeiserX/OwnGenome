Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer

BRCA2c.5946delp.Ser1982ArgfsTer22chr13:32340301 GT>Gone copy (heterozygous)

One copy of BRCA2, a gene that repairs broken DNA, has lost a single letter. From that point the gene is read out of step, and the protein stops about 1,400 building blocks short. People with such a change have a higher lifetime risk of breast and ovarian cancer. It is not a diagnosis of cancer.

Words usedFrameshift: a deletion that shifts how the gene is read, so the protein is cut short.

Heterozygous: on one of the two copies.

What to do

In this order. None of it is urgent this week; all of it is worth doing.
  1. Bring this page to a doctor or a genetic counsellor.A counsellor explains what it means for Elena and her relatives.
  2. Confirm it with a clinical test in a certified laboratory.This pipeline is research; a clinic will not act on it alone.
  3. Ask about screening and prevention.Breast screening with MRI, and options for the ovaries, start earlier than the general programme.
  4. Tell close relatives they can be tested.Parents, brothers, sisters and children each have a 1 in 2 chance of carrying it.
