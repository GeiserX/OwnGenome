# Secondary Findings and Carrier Status

Part of [Interpreting Your Results](../interpreting-results.md). Read [Before You Look](../interpreting-results.md#before-you-look-what-the-pipeline-can-tell-you) first.

## Carrier Status Is Not Disease

The most common "pathogenic" finding in any genome is **heterozygous carrier status for recessive conditions**. This means:

- You have ONE copy of a variant that causes disease when BOTH copies are affected
- For most recessive conditions, heterozygous carriers are **not clinically affected** (though some carrier states confer subtle phenotypic effects — e.g., sickle cell trait, HFE carriers and iron loading)
- The primary relevance is for **family planning**: if your partner carries the same gene, each child has a 25% chance of being affected
- **Note — MUTYH**: Biallelic (homozygous or compound het) MUTYH carriers have a well-established high colorectal cancer risk. For **monoallelic** (single-copy) carriers, the evidence is more nuanced: some meta-analyses show a modest risk increase, but a counseling framework for moderate-penetrance CRC genes (Genetics in Medicine) notes that risk estimates for monoallelic MUTYH are conflicting and that screening recommendations (e.g., earlier colonoscopy) were historically tied to carriers with a CRC family history. Discuss with a genetic counselor, especially if you have a family history of CRC
- Examples: GJB2 (hearing loss), CFTR (cystic fibrosis), HFE (hemochromatosis)

**Your own carrier list (step 24 report).** The ClinVar card of both reports is followed by a carrier list for this one sample, with no partner needed: every step 6 ClinVar hit in the 3,292 recessive and X-linked genes of `assets/carrier_genes.tsv` (the list of the [couple screen](../39-couple.md#the-carrier-list)). An allele that list marks as a hypomorph (for example BTD p.Asp444His or the GALT Duarte variants) or a pseudodeficiency allele (HEXA c.739C>T and c.745C>T, which lower the enzyme test result but cause no Tay-Sachs disease) is listed as set aside, never as a carrier finding on its own. A hit that is a combined allele of step 6 or a part of one keeps the note the ClinVar table prints for it (`combined_allele` in `summary.json`), and so does a secondary finding: an unphased combined allele says its parts may sit on different copies.

Each row of the list keeps its zygosity (`zygosity`: `het`, `hom`, `hemizygous`, or `unknown` when no alt copy is called) and step 6's call quality: `gq`, `dp` and `low_confidence`. The mark is judged against the floor the hits file states (`##PGP_CALL_FLOOR`, GQ 30 and DP 10 by default), so it is the same mark the ClinVar table shows for the hit. A carrier has one changed copy and one unchanged copy. A row called homozygous, or hemizygous (on a male's X, or a hit called homozygous inside a deletion), has no unchanged copy, so it is not a carrier finding: `summary.json` lists it apart under `hom_or_hemizygous`. Both reports still print it in the carrier list, on a line of its own that ends "two changed copies, not the pattern of a carrier" or "the only copy changed, not the pattern of a carrier", and the list's first line counts it apart from the carriers ("6 finding(s), 2 with no unchanged copy"). The live page keeps its finding for it, and its carrier headline asks for attention. Two different heterozygous hits in one recessive gene are counted as copies, with the rule of the secondary-findings card below. Read-backed phase on different copies gives the field `pair` the value `two hits`. Unknown phase gives `possible two hits`. Both on one copy (cis) gives no `pair`: one copy is hit. The `phase` field says why. The hits are counted twice: all of them, and the confident ones alone. A pair the confident hits prove stays `two hits`, even when another hit of the gene is a low-confidence call. When the confident hits count two copies but their phase is unknown, the pair is `possible two hits`, even when a low-confidence call would prove it. Any other pair that needs a low-confidence call is `possible two hits (low-confidence call)`. A combined allele counts as confident when any call of it is, and its phase then comes from that confident call, in `pair` and beside a deletion in `two_hits`. `pair` counts heterozygous hits only: a homozygous row or a deletion is not part of it. The secondary-findings card below counts the same way. For a recessive gene that the card counts by two copies (MUTYH, ATP7B, CYP27A1, GAA, RPE65, CASQ2, TRDN, and BTD apart from its hypomorph), the two lists give the gene the same answer. On the card that answer is its `pair`. Here it is the best of `pair`, the gene's `two_hits` verdict and its `hom_or_hemizygous` rows. `two hits` here is `biallelic` there, `possible two hits` is `possibly biallelic`, and the two low-confidence words match. Rare shapes read more cautiously on the card. A combined allele that starts inside a deletion and ends past it, in trans with it by read-backed phase, is `possibly biallelic` there, because the card judges it at its start, a heterozygous call inside the deletion. Here its part past the deletion makes it `biallelic` in `two_hits`. A weak homozygous call of a combined allele, a confident heterozygous call of a part of it and a deletion give the low-confidence word there and `possibly biallelic` here. A gene with another rule on the card, such as HFE or the X-linked ABCD1, can read differently on the two lists. The card also reads the HIGH-impact calls of the clinical VCF, and the list does not. So the card can count a call the list does not have: a HIGH-impact variant that is no ClinVar hit, or the HIGH-impact call of a part of a combined allele, which makes that allele confident on the card while the list marks it low confidence. When the clinical VCF states a lower call floor than the hits file, a call can also be low confidence on the list and confident on the card. A HIGH-impact call that is weak on allele balance alone never lowers a hit whose ClinVar row is confident. The reports do not show `zygosity`, `gq`, `dp`, `low_confidence`, `pair` or `phase` yet.

**Secondary findings follow each gene's ACMG rule.** The secondary-findings card (step 23's section of the report; in summary.json also `sections.acmg_sf`, written from step 6's hits on every run, VEP cache or not) applies the reporting rule of each of the 84 ACMG SF v3.3 genes (Lee et al., Genet Med 2025;27(8):101454, Table 1), written gene by gene in `assets/acmg_sf.tsv`:

- a dominant gene (BRCA1, LDLR, MYH7 and most others): one pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant is reported;
- a recessive gene (MUTYH, ATP7B, BTD, CYP27A1, GAA, RPE65, CASQ2, TRDN): both copies of the gene must be hit. A homozygote, or two variants that read-backed phase puts on different copies (a `|` genotype with the same phase set, PS), are biallelic. Two different variants of unknown phase are still reported, as possibly biallelic: most short-read calls carry no phase, and they may sit on one copy. In `summary.json` each such finding has a `pair` field (`biallelic` or `possibly biallelic`) and a `phase` field that says why. The card counts the variants twice, with every call and with the confident calls alone. A pair that needs a low-confidence call is `possibly biallelic (low-confidence call)`: confirm that call first. A low-confidence call is one below step 6's call floor (its `low_confidence` mark), or a weak HIGH-impact call (a HIGH-impact record's `weak_call` mark: below the clinical call floor, or an allele balance out of range). A variant counts as confident when any call of it is: its ClinVar row or its HIGH-impact record. A confident homozygous call, a confident pair or a confident deletion pair keeps `biallelic` even when another variant of the gene is a low-confidence call, and two confident variants of unknown phase stay `possibly biallelic`, even when a low-confidence call would prove the pair. A combined allele counts as confident when any call of it is. Then it counts with the copies, GT and phase set of that confident call. Two variants that read-backed phase puts on the same copy (cis) hit one copy only, so they are listed apart with the phase set. A `|` genotype with no phase set, or a half call such as `1|.`, counts as phase unknown. The two alt alleles of one split call (`1/.` and `./1` at one position) sit on different copies. A combined allele of step 6 counts once with its parts, unless it is unphased and two of its parts are hits on their own. A record with no alt copy called counts nothing. One heterozygous MUTYH variant is listed apart as not reported, with the reason;
- HFE: only homozygous p.Cys282Tyr. A heterozygous p.Cys282Tyr or a homozygous p.His63Asp is not reported;
- X-linked: GLA (Fabry disease) and OTC are reported in a hemizygous male and a heterozygous female alike. ABCD1 (X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy) is reported hemizygous, homozygous or with two heterozygous variants, never for one heterozygous variant;
- BTD with p.Asp444His: reported as partial, not profound, biotinidase deficiency;
- AnnotSV class 4 and 5 structural variants (step 5) in BRCA1, BRCA2, MLH1, MSH2 and LDLR reach the same card, and so does a deletion of the 3' end of EPCAM (exons 8 and 9), which silences MSH2 (Kovacs et al., Hum Mutat 2009;30(2):197-203).

**Two hits across callers: a deletion plus a variant on the other copy.** Small-variant callers see one hit when the other copy of the gene is deleted. So the carrier list also joins the deletions the other engines call with the ClinVar hits, gene by gene (`og summary collect`, `two_hits`). The deletions come from AnnotSV's class 4 and 5 deletions (step 5), step 35's interval table (the recurrent regions of `bin/cn_loci.tsv`, such as 1q21.1), its HBA1/HBA2 count and Parascopy's SMN1 count. A recessive gene that a deletion and a hit both reach gets one line:

- **biallelic** when the reads show the two hits on different copies. Either the hit is called homozygous inside the deletion, so it sits on the one copy left (the line and the carrier list call it hemizygous; if the deletion is a false call, the hit is homozygous and both copies still carry it), or both calls carry read-backed phase (a `|` genotype with the same phase set, PS) on different copies. A low-expression (hypomorph) allele called homozygous inside a deletion is only **possibly biallelic**: it causes disease with a null allele only if the deletion is real, so confirm the deletion (array or MLPA);
- **possibly biallelic** otherwise: the phase is unknown. Most short-read calls carry no phase, so this is the usual line. Step 05's checked table keeps no genotype column yet, and step 35's counts have no phase, so in a pipeline run a deletion pairs as possibly biallelic unless the hit is called homozygous inside it. A parent's genome (step 38, [family analysis](../multi-sample.md#family-analysis-step-38-opt-in)) or long reads can settle it;
- **possibly biallelic (low-confidence call)** when the pair would count but the small variant's call is low confidence: below step 6's call floor (its `low_confidence` mark), or on the card also a weak HIGH-impact call. Confirm the call first. A low-confidence call of a combined allele pairs by a confident call of the same allele (the combined call or a part of it) when that call gives as many copies, with that call's GT and phase set;
- no line when read-backed phase puts both on the same copy (cis): the other copy is intact.

Examples: an exon deletion of MUTYH plus a pathogenic MUTYH variant; three alpha-globin genes plus an HBA2 point variant; one SMN1 copy plus an SMN1 variant (short reads place few SMN1 variants reliably, so check with a targeted test); and the 1q21.1 deletion plus one of the two RBM8A low-expression alleles (c.-21G>A, rs139428292, or c.67+32G>C, rs201779890), the cause of TAR syndrome (thrombocytopenia with absent radius; Albers et al., Nat Genet 2012;44(4):435-439, PMID 22366785). On its own an RBM8A low-expression allele stays set aside. The [couple screen](../39-couple.md) (step 39) applies the same pairing between partners: a hypomorph allele of the list in one partner and a full loss of function in the same gene in the other (a ClinVar pathogenic variant, a Delly deletion) is a shared gene with its risk for a child; the allele in both partners, or in one alone, stays set aside. In the secondary-findings card a called deletion counts as one of the two variants a recessive gene needs, unless read-backed phase puts it on the variant's copy. The line under the carrier list names the deletion callers it read and the ones it could not read. A deletion AnnotSV ranks below class 4 is not counted.

Without a readable clinical VCF from step 23 (no VEP cache, step 23 skipped or failed with no VCF left, or a VCF that cannot be read) the card in `sections.acmg_sf` holds the ClinVar hits and the SVs only, and its `high_impact_note` says so: a predicted loss-of-function variant that is not in ClinVar is not screened, alone or as the second hit of a recessive gene. A clinical VCF left by an earlier run is still read when step 23 failed in this run, as the clinical section reads it; that section then shows as stale, and the card's `high_impact_note` gives the date of that earlier VCF and step 23's result in this run.

The TTN rule (truncating variants only) and the LMNA rule (missense only with cardiac evidence) are printed beside the finding, not applied. It is still a list to review with a clinician.

**The disease ClinVar names can differ from the ACMG one.** A gene can cause more than one disease, and the card finds a ClinVar hit by its gene. RET causes MEN2, the disease ACMG lists it for, and also Hirschsprung disease. So each ClinVar finding in `summary.json` keeps the condition ClinVar names for the variant (`clinvar_condition`, with its database ids in `clinvar_condition_ids`) beside the disease ACMG lists (`acmg_phenotype`). When every condition named is another disease, such as a RET variant reported only for Hirschsprung disease, the finding has `condition_not_acmg: true`. It stays on the card: check which disease the variant was reported for before you read it as a MEN2 finding. Generic names such as "not provided" or "Hereditary cancer-predisposing syndrome" never set the mark. The carrier list keeps ClinVar's condition on each row the same way. The reports do not show these fields yet.

## CPSR Report (Step 17)

**What it tells you:** Cancer predisposition screening using CPSR's curated cancer gene panels (panel 0 covers 500+ genes). Step 17 also turns on CPSR's secondary findings: the ACMG SF list, which includes cardiac and metabolic genes outside cancer (see [Before you look](../interpreting-results.md#before-you-look-what-the-pipeline-can-tell-you)).

**Turning CPSR's secondary findings off.** Set `CPSR_SECONDARY_FINDINGS=false` before the run: `CPSR_SECONDARY_FINDINGS=false ./scripts/run-all.sh <sample> <male|female>`, or the same before `./scripts/17-cpsr.sh <sample>`. A direct `nextflow run` takes `--cpsr_secondary_findings false`. CPSR then reports the cancer genes only, with no secondary-findings section. `run-all.sh` keeps the switch in `logs/run_status.tsv`, so `og recheck --rerun` keeps it off. The switch does not touch the secondary-findings card of the report (step 23, above): OwnGenome computes that card from the step 6 ClinVar hits on every run, plus step 23's rare HIGH-impact records and step 5's class 4 and 5 SVs when those steps ran, so it can still list a variant in an ACMG SF gene. Do not open that card if you do not want to see such findings.

**Where to look:** `${SAMPLE}/cpsr/` — open the HTML report in a browser.

**How to read it:**
- CPSR puts every variant in its genes into one of the five ACMG/AMP classes: **Pathogenic**, **Likely pathogenic**, **VUS** (uncertain significance), **Likely benign** and **Benign**. A variant that already has a ClinVar entry can carry ClinVar's class instead of the one CPSR computed.
- Only **Pathogenic** and **Likely pathogenic** call for clinical attention. The report lists them first, and the secondary findings in their own section.

## Clinical Filter (Step 23)

**Where to look:** `${SAMPLE}/clinical/${SAMPLE}_clinical_summary.tsv`, one row per variant with gene, impact, scores and gnomAD constraint.

Step 23 keeps the HIGH-impact and MODERATE variants below 1% in every population VEP reports (`MAX_AF`), the HIGH and MODERATE ones with no known frequency (in a tier of their own), the ClinVar pathogenic ones at any frequency and, when step 30 ran, the variants that CADD, SpliceAI, REVEL or AlphaMissense flag. Expect a few hundred rows. Most are heterozygous variants in genes that tolerate one broken copy. Read the rows in constrained genes (low LOEUF, see [gene constraint](clinvar.md#gnomad-gene-constraint-step-23-summary)) first, then the homozygous ones. A row of unknown frequency is not rare, so check gnomAD for it before you worry. [Step 23](../23-clinical-filter.md#what-gets-filtered) gives the exact rules.

## Variant Prioritization (Step 31)

**Where to look:** `${SAMPLE}/slivar/${SAMPLE}_slivar_summary.tsv`, `${SAMPLE}/slivar/${SAMPLE}_compound_hets.tsv` and `${SAMPLE}/slivar/${SAMPLE}_compound_het_pairs.tsv`.

slivar sorts the damaging variants into four groups (rare HIGH, rare MODERATE with damaging scores, ClinVar pathogenic, and frequency unknown: HIGH or MODERATE with no population frequency, so not called rare) and lists genes where you carry two such variants. The genotypes alone cannot tell whether the two variants sit on different copies of the gene (in trans, which can cause recessive disease) or on the same copy (in cis, which usually does not), so step 31 reads the phase from the reads: `${SAMPLE}_compound_het_pairs.tsv` says `trans`, `cis` or `unknown` for each pair, with the reads that say so. Only variants close enough for one read pair to cover both (a few hundred bases) can be phased this way, so most pairs read `unknown`. Expect a thousand or more candidate pairs; nearly all are noise. A `trans` pair matters only in a gene that fits your health history, and an `unknown` one needs a parent's DNA or long reads to phase. See [step 31](../31-slivar.md#interpretation).
